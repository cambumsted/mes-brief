Marketing Effectiveness Solution

A single place where DDS leadership can answer any question about how our marketing is performing — investment, trends, campaigns, and partnerships — at a glance, with the detail one click away.

The problem: Our channels are siloed and our reporting is scattered. No one place tells leadership "here is how marketing is doing right now, and where to invest next."

01 · The Problem

Leadership has no single source for marketing performance.

Today, DDS leadership relies on a patchwork of meetings, decks, and channel-specific reports to understand how marketing is performing — and for some channels, ROI visibility is limited or missing altogether. Each channel — paid media, email, partnerships, SEO, GTM — operates and reports in its own silo, and no view stitches them together.

What's broken today
  • Leadership can't easily see where marketing dollars should go next — there's no portfolio-level view to guide investment decisions.
  • Each channel — paid media, email, partnerships, SEO — operates and reports in its own silo, with no view stitching them together.
  • Regional partnership performance (Asia, Americas, EMEA) is reported in silos and rarely reaches leadership.
  • No comparable view across partnerships — variables differ, so investment decisions lack a shared baseline.
  • Outbound marketing activities (emails, paid media, partnerships, events) aren't tied back to the GTM moments they support — like Back-to-School or Cyber Week — so we can't see how the full push performed against the moment it was built for.
What this costs us
  • Reactive investment decisions instead of portfolio-level optimization.
  • Partnership dollars allocated by inertia, not evidence.
  • Leadership questions could be answered faster, with less back-and-forth across teams.
  • Marketing's contribution to GTM moments is undersold internally.
02 · Guiding Principles

How we'll know we built the right thing.

High-level by default, drill-down on demand

Headlines for skim-readers. Detail for the curious. Both audiences served from the same surface.

Comparable across silos

Especially partnerships across regions. Different goals are fine; missing comparability is not.

Focused on investable spend

This solution is about the costs leadership actively decides on — like funding an ad campaign — not operational line items like creative template updates.

Built to be used, not admired

Adoption by leadership is the success metric. If it's not in the flow of decisions, we got it wrong.

03 · Questions This Solution Answers

Four pillars, four questions.

Pillar 1

Investment & ROI

Question: Are we getting return on the marketing dollars we're spending?

e.g., "Did Back-to-School paid media beat its ROI target this quarter?"
Pillar 2

Marketing Performance Trends

Question: How is marketing doing right now?

Today this lives in the MBR; we want this information front and center, continuously available rather than waiting for the next review.

e.g., "Email engagement is down two months running — is that a leading indicator?"
Pillar 3

Campaign Performance

Question: What did marketing contribute to a given GTM moment?

Today GTM owns campaign assets and promotion coordination, but no view rolls up "Outbound Marketing drove $X for Back-to-School." We want to bridge tactical execution to outcome attribution.

e.g., "Which paid media campaigns contributed most to Back-to-School?"
Pillar 4

Partnership Performance

Question: Which partnership is delivering the best ROI?

e.g., "Across all regions, which partnerships drove the strongest pipeline last quarter?"

05 · Ideal State

What "good" looks like.

Pillar 1 · Investment & ROI

Capability: A unified view of marketing investment & return for all channels.

Drill-down: from channel → campaign → individual partnership.

Pillar 2 · Performance Trends

Capability: An always-on "headline" view of marketing health by channel — refreshed continuously, not monthly.

Pillar 3 · Campaign Performance

Capability: Roll-up of marketing's contribution to each GTM moment, attributing channel and campaign back to the moment it served.

Drill-down: from moment (e.g., Back-to-School) → contributing channels → individual campaign assets.

Pillar 4 · Partnership Performance

Capability: A cross-region view of partnership performance that surfaces what's working, where to invest more, and where to reconsider.

Drill-down: from portfolio view → individual partnership → regional context.

06 · Gaps & Enhancements Needed

What we need to close to reach the ideal state.

Data Gaps

  • No attribution layer linking campaigns to channels and to outbound marketing KPIs for GTM moments.
  • Partnership KPIs not standardized across regions.

Process Gaps

  • Regional partnership reporting never aggregates upward.

Tooling Gaps

  • No shared layer that pulls MBR + campaign + partnership data into one place.
  • Existing reports are slide-based and not navigable on demand.

Ownership Gaps

  • No single owner for cross-channel performance synthesis.
  • Partnership portfolio view has no accountable owner today.

08 · Phased Roadmap

Crawl, walk, run.

Three phases, roughly 10–12 months end to end. Each phase delivers usable value on its own — we don't wait until "Run" to put something in leadership's hands. See the full sequencing on the workstream timeline.

Phase 1 · Crawl

Define & align

~6–8 weeks

Get to a shared baseline leadership can reference, even if it's manually assembled.

  • Reports inventory across all four pillars — owners, cadence, gaps.
  • Metrics dictionary — define each KPI once: name, calculation, owner, source.
  • Partnership grid template standardized across regions.
  • Manual v1 of the unified view — curated, not automated.
Phase exitOne curated view referenced in MBR, plus an agreed dictionary and grid template.
Phase 2 · Walk

Centralize & standardize

~3–4 months

Move from a curated artifact to an operational rhythm leadership relies on.

  • Landing experience pilot — single page linking existing reports.
  • Operationalize the partnership grid with regional contributors on cadence.
  • Cross-pillar review cadence that drives a real decision each cycle.
  • Campaign-to-moment attribution v1 — tag outbound activity to GTM moments.
Phase exitLive landing experience used in a recurring leadership rhythm, with the grid maintained regionally.
Phase 3 · Run

Integrate & automate

~4–6 months

Make the solution leadership's first stop and the named source of truth.

  • Integrated solution with drill-downs across all four pillars.
  • Automate moment attribution where data supports it; keep human review for ambiguity.
  • Continuous trend view replaces MBR-only cadence.
  • Embedded in leadership decision rituals — adoption is the success metric.
Phase exitSolution named as the source of truth in MBR, planning, and investment conversations.
09 · Success Metrics

How we'll know it worked.

Leading

  • % of leadership questions answerable from one place
  • Time-to-answer for a typical leadership question
  • % of partnerships represented in the grid

Lagging

  • Investment decisions informed by the solution
  • Partnership reallocation actions taken on the basis of the grid
  • Campaign-to-moment ROI claims made externally with evidence

Adoption

  • Leadership engagement frequency
  • Mentions in leadership meetings as the source-of-truth
  • Self-service usage vs. analyst-pulled reports
10 · Risks, Dependencies & Open Questions

What could derail this.

Risks

  • "Another dashboard nobody uses." Mitigated by embedding in leadership rituals from Phase 1.
  • Regional partnership data standardization is a people/process problem, not just tooling.
  • Channel leads may resist a comparable view that exposes weaker performance.
  • ROI definition disagreements between Marketing and Finance could stall Pillar 1.

Dependencies

  • Finance — for shared ROI definitions.
  • GTM — for canonical campaign and moment taxonomy.
  • Marketing analytics / BI — for any data integration.
  • Regional partnership leads — for grid contributions.

Open Questions

  • Who owns the solution end-to-end?
  • What's the right cadence for the "headline" trend view — weekly, daily, continuous?
  • Should SEO be a fifth pillar or stay nested under Performance Trends?
  • How do we handle partnerships that don't have a clean ROI tie?
  • Where does this live organizationally — Marketing analytics, GTM, or a new function?
