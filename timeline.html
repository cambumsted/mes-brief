Workstream Timeline

A reasonable path from where we are today to an integrated marketing effectiveness solution — sequenced through the Crawl, Walk, Run framework with rough time estimates per workstream. Dates are illustrative; actual sequencing depends on resourcing and stakeholder alignment.

At a glance

Three phases, roughly 10–12 months end to end. Each phase delivers usable value on its own — we don't wait until "Run" to put something in leadership's hands.

Phase 1

Crawl — Define & Align

~6–8 weeks

Discover what stakeholders need, inventory what exists, and stand up a manual MVP of the unified view.

Phase 2

Walk — Centralize & Standardize

~3–4 months

Update OCID classifications, operationalize the partnership grid, and begin moment-level attribution.

Phase 3

Run — Integrate & Automate

~4–6 months

Integrated solution with drill-downs, automation where ROI justifies it, embedded in leadership rituals.

Phase 1 · Crawl — Define & Align

Get to a shared baseline

~6–8 weeks Goal: leadership can see one curated view of marketing performance, even if it's manually assembled.
WorkstreamDurationKey activities
Discovery period May – Jul Reach out to stakeholders across DDS Marketing, channel leads, GTM, and finance partners. Align on wish-list items, understand what each audience wants out of a unified marketing destination, and surface the questions they're trying to answer today. Output is a synthesized requirements list that anchors everything that follows.
Compile reports inventory 2–3 weeks Catalog existing reports across all channels and pillars. Identify owners, refresh cadence, and audience. Flag duplicates and gaps so we know what we're working with before building anything new.
Tracking education (OCID & CMM) 3–4 weeks Understand how OCIDs are created and tracked at Microsoft today — who owns the request process, how the CMM hierarchy is structured, and which parameters (channel, campaign, source, sub-channel, etc.) are available to leverage. Document the existing taxonomy, where it has drifted from how we actually market (e.g., PMAX still rolling up under SEM), and what should change in Walk. This becomes the input for the Walk-phase reclassification.
Partnership grid template (v1) 2–3 weeks Standardize the cross-region partnership KPI grid. Agree on minimum comparable fields. Pressure-test with 2–3 partnerships before rolling regionally.
Manual Unified View MVP Jul – Sep Curated deck or page that pulls headlines from each pillar. Refreshed by hand. Proves the format and the read-out before any tooling investment, and gives leadership something to react to early.
Stakeholder & RACI Ongoing Confirm working group, owners per pillar, and decision rights. Lock the RACI before Walk starts.
Phase 1 exit: leadership has a Manual Unified View MVP they can reference, a documented OCID/CMM landscape ready to be reclassified, and an agreed partnership grid template.

Phase 2 · Walk — Centralize & Standardize

Move from curated artifact to operational rhythm

~3–4 months Goal: a real landing surface, regional contributors filling the grid on cadence, first moment-level attribution.
WorkstreamDurationKey activities
Update OCID classifications 8–10 weeks Apply what we learned in the Crawl-phase deep dive: reclassify channels and sub-channels in the CMM hierarchy so reporting reflects how we actually market. Examples: split PMAX out from SEM as its own channel, formalize partnerships as a top-level classification, and align email/paid media taxonomies. Coordinate with the OCID request owners so all new requests follow the updated structure.
Operationalize partnership grid Quarterly cadence Regional contributors (Asia, Americas, EMEA) update the grid each quarter. Establish a QA loop. First fully comparable cross-region partnership view that leadership can rely on.
Cross-pillar review cadence Ongoing (start ~Sep) Recurring working session that uses the Manual Unified View to drive a real decision each cycle. Tests whether the surface is actually decision-ready before investing in build.
Campaign-to-moment attribution (v1) 8–12 weeks Tag outbound activities (email, paid, partnerships, GTM) to the moments they support — Back-to-School, Holiday, Cyber Week, etc. Manual tagging is fine in v1; the goal is proving the roll-up works before automating.
ROI visibility gap-fix 4–6 weeks Identify channels with weak or missing ROI signal. For upper- and mid-funnel channels where ROI isn't the goal, define the expected outcome (e.g., qualified traffic, engaged sessions) so every channel has something to be measured against.
Phase 2 exit: updated OCID classifications live in CMM, the partnership grid is on a quarterly cadence with regional contributors, and a first pass at moment-level attribution is rolled up in the Manual Unified View.

Phase 3 · Run — Integrate & Automate

Make it the source of truth

~4–6 months Goal: integrated solution with drill-downs, selective automation, and a permanent place in leadership decision rituals.
WorkstreamDurationKey activities
Integrated solution build 3–5 months Move from links-and-curation to integrated drill-downs across the four pillars. Form follows requirements — dashboard, hub, or hybrid.
Automate moment attribution 6–8 weeks Replace manual tagging with system-side rules where the data supports it. Keep human review for ambiguous attribution.
Continuous trend view 4–6 weeks Replace MBR-only cadence with always-on headline trends. Alerting on directional shifts before they become surprises.
Embed in leadership rituals Ongoing Solution is referenced by name in MBR, planning, and investment decisions. Adoption — not output — becomes the success metric.
Selective automation Continuous Automate where the ROI of automation is justified. Leave manual processes manual where they're cheaper than the integration cost.
Phase 3 exit: the solution is leadership's first stop for marketing performance questions and is named as the source of truth in decision conversations.

Assumptions & caveats

  • Time estimates assume a small dedicated working group (not a full project team) with leadership air cover.
  • Phases overlap: Walk-phase work on the partnership grid begins while Crawl-phase definition work is still finalizing.
  • Run-phase build duration depends heavily on the technology choice — kept intentionally rough until that decision is made.
  • Dates assume no major reorg, no competing executive initiative pulling the same stakeholders, and access to existing reporting owners.
  • Each phase is designed to deliver standalone value — if priorities shift, we don't lose what's been built.
