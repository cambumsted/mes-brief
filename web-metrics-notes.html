Topic · Creative ↔ GTM moment ↔ OCID

Can we connect GTM moments (Back to School, Holiday, etc.) to OCIDs so we can see what creative is running where without manual pulls?

Question (to Jordan) Asked about capabilities to track creative placements by GTM Moment (e.g. Back to School, Holiday) — essentially, is there a way to connect GTM moments to OCIDs so we don't need manual data pulls to understand what's running where.
Response (Jordan) — TL;DR It's not possible to get to creative messaging via OCID strings in today's (Q4) set-up. The primary reason is how dynamic our creative rotation is across SEM/PMAX, Display, Social, and OLV. OCID strings are generated once per quarter after all new channels and tactics have run — they don't continuously generate.

Channel-by-channel breakdown

Channel Why OCID-by-creative doesn't work today Alternative source for creative-level read
SEM / PMAX
Google & MSA		 We don't traffic by SEM copy. Copy is dynamically served and auto-optimized across evergreen, promos, and other variants. Back-end performance by creative type as a proxy, pulled directly from the platforms.
Display
The Trade Desk + Adobe rotations		 Creative ID is just "Adobe." All creative / banners are dynamically rotated by Adobe (24–48 hr swap window — Mylan and Priscilla know this well). Back-end performance through Adobe — the taxonomy lets us map which creative performed best.
OLV
Sunday Sky		 Same dynamic-serve pattern — no OCIDs are generated by message. Back-end metrics through Sunday Sky.
Social Same — copy and message are swapped in/out dynamically on the platform. Platform back-end by creative variant.
Path forward: A solve may be possible going forward, but we'll likely have to get creative with proxies. OCID strings are not inherently dynamic — generation is a manual quarterly process, so they can't be the primary join key for creative-level reporting.

Implications for the unified view

  • Don't promise creative-level cuts in V1 via OCID. Use platform back-ends as the source of truth for creative performance.
  • For GTM moment views, lean on tactic / campaign-level aggregations + manual moment tagging until a dynamic taxonomy exists.
  • Open question: what would a proxy-based "moment → creative" join look like, and who owns it?

Notes from email conversations on transactional email tracking, attribution, and segmentation.

Discovery meeting · Courtney & Emma · 5/12/2026

Tracking and attribution of transactional email traffic

Discussed the need for improved tracking and attribution of traffic and revenue generated by specific transactional emails — abandoned order, Xbox wishlist, and purchase receipt. Emma flagged the lack of an evergreen report and the difficulty of crediting this traffic within existing business review frameworks.

What we want to achieve

  • Evergreen visibility into transactional email performance — sessions and orders attributable to specific emails (purchase receipt, abandoned order, Xbox wishlist, welcome) so the channel doesn't keep falling through the cracks of standard reporting.
  • Diagnostic use of the data — better visibility helps explain trends in revenue and site visits (e.g., spikes in email activity correlated with shifts in customer behavior).
  • Segment-level insight — slice abandoned-order email by product category (gaming vs. Surface, etc.) so promotional content in those emails can be tailored to whichever products are driving engagement.
  • Cross-LOB credit — Emma's work spans multiple categories; the existing reporting buckets don't cleanly accommodate that. Need a way to attribute credit for cross-LOB transactional email activity.
  • Integrate transactional email with the rest of the traffic-channel data (similar to how paid channels are segmented) so email's contribution to overall site traffic and conversions reads cleanly alongside the other channels.

Recent data we reviewed

  • Recent week: purchase receipt email — 5,285 sessions / 114 orders.
  • The promotional banner inside the purchase receipt email contributed $3,300 in revenue tied back to abandoned-order email behavior — tangible proof these placements move the number.
  • Noted some discrepancies between the new report and Power BI; difference is within an acceptable range, but reinforces the need for a single consistent source of truth for this view.

Follow-up tasks

  • Emma + Cam: compile a list of questions about transactional email traffic and the data insights we'd like to get out of available reporting tools — use this to drive what the evergreen report needs to support.
  • Cam: integrate transactional and welcome email traffic data alongside the rest of the traffic-channel data for comprehensive, comparable analysis.
  • Emma + Cam: iterate on segmentation (abandoned order by product category) and reporting so insights are actionable and email gets proper recognition cross-LOB.
